Transforming Waste into Urban Infrastructure

Before applying the materials widely, we will conduct extensive tests on roads, bike lanes, and walking paths. These tests include load-bearing capacity, resistance to cracking, and durability under different weather conditions. Using 20% recycled textile fibers in the mixture can help recycle approximately 5,000 tons of textile waste per year, significantly reducing landfill contributions while creating strong and sustainable urban surfaces.

Testing and Recycling Impact

The project also encourages community involvement. Citizens can contribute by donating old clothes, and local authorities can track the amount of recycled textiles. Collaboration with companies and municipal organizations ensures that this initiative can scale, turning waste into a valuable resource and promoting sustainable urban development.

Community Engagement and Collaboration

Our project transforms textile waste into durable materials that can be used in city infrastructure. Instead of ending up in landfills, old clothes and fabrics are shredded and mixed with road construction materials. This creates surfaces that are strong enough for cars, bicycles, and pedestrians, while reducing environmental pollution caused by textile waste.

Community Impact

Engaging local communities encourages awareness of recycling and sustainable urban development.
